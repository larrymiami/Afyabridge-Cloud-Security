## Governed state machine

The reviewed lifecycle is:

```text
open
  |\
  | +-----------------> risk-accepted
  |                         |
  v                         v
in-remediation <------------+
  |
  v
resolved
  |\
  | +-- verification failed --> in-remediation
  |
  +---- verification passed --> closed
```

`closed` is terminal. A recurrence after closure becomes a new finding so old audit history remains immutable.

## Severity and remediation deadlines

Finding severity is inherited from the active v0.10A control catalogue and cannot be downgraded in the finding record. The existing governance SLA remains authoritative:

| Severity | Maximum remediation time | Merge blocking posture severity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Critical | 24 hours | yes |
| High | 168 hours / 7 days | yes |
| Medium | 720 hours / 30 days | no |
| Low | 2160 hours / 90 days | no |

`remediation_due_at` cannot exceed the severity limit. Open or in-remediation findings past the deadline fail lifecycle validation. Risk acceptance cannot be approved after the original remediation deadline, preventing a late exception from retroactively erasing an SLA breach.

The lifecycle schema recognizes:

- `repository-posture`;
- `manual-review`;
- `terraform-drift`; and
- `security-command-center`.

Only `repository-posture` and `manual-review` are currently active for operational findings.

`terraform-drift` remains inactive until a plan is produced against deployed remote state in a reviewed drift mode. `security-command-center` remains inactive until the live Google Cloud foundation and SCC integration exist. Synthetic tests prove these planned sources cannot be used to fabricate operational findings today.

## Risk acceptance and exception linkage

Risk acceptance uses the existing `SEC-EX-YYYY-NNN` registry, but the authorization is bound to the **specific `risk-accepted` history event** rather than a mutable top-level field on the finding.

Each risk-acceptance event must:

- name its exact exception ID;
- be explicitly listed in that exception's `finding_ids`;
- match the exception owner;
- use an approved independent exception approver;
- use the source-appropriate exception gate;
- map to a control that permits exceptions;
- remain within both global and control-specific maximum lifetime; and
- occur inside that exception's authorization window.

The reviewed source-to-gate mapping is:

| Finding source | Required exception gate |
|---|---|
| repository-posture | cloud-posture |
| manual-review | cloud-posture |
| security-command-center | cloud-posture |
| terraform-drift | terraform-drift |

A legacy top-level `exception_id` is rejected, and exception IDs are rejected on non-risk lifecycle events.

### Active vs historical semantics

A currently risk-accepted finding requires the exception on its terminal risk-acceptance event to be `active` and unexpired **now**.

After that acceptance period ends, the exception can be retired as `historical`. Historical entries remain in `security/exceptions.json` with `retired_on`, preserving the approval record even after its original expiry date.

For a historical risk event, the validator proves the event occurred between exception creation and the earlier of retirement/expiry. The exception does not need to remain active at the present validation date.

This allows audit history to remain valid without permitting stale approvals to authorize current risk.

### Audit contradiction fixed during review

An earlier reviewer iteration correctly taught the finding validator that an old closed finding should remain valid after its historical exception expired, but the global exception validator still rejected every expired exception. That made the design internally impossible: retaining the record failed global validation, while deleting it broke finding linkage.

The final model resolves the contradiction by retaining retired approvals as governed `historical` entries in the same registry.

### Multiple acceptance periods fixed during review

The earlier design stored one top-level `exception_id` on a finding. A finding can legitimately move from risk acceptance back to remediation and later receive a new, separately approved exception. A single mutable field would overwrite or invalidate the first approval.

The final model binds each risk-accepted history event to its own exception ID. A dedicated positive test proves two distinct acceptance periods can retain separate exception records while the earlier approval is already historical.

## Resolution attempts and failed verification

Every transition into `resolved` creates exactly one immutable `resolution_attempts` entry:

- a current resolved attempt is `pending` independent verification;
- failed verification records independent verifier, timestamp, and evidence, then requires transition back to `in-remediation`;
- the failed attempt remains in the registry;
- a later transition to `resolved` creates a new attempt; and
- only the terminal attempt may record `passed` verification and close the finding.

The test suite demonstrates both first-attempt closure and a failed-first-attempt / successful-second-attempt path.

This prevents a failed verification from being deleted or overwritten merely to make the finding advance.

## Closure integrity

A closed finding requires:

- final verification status `passed`;
- verifier from the approved governance owners;
- verifier independent from the finding owner;
- verification after the corresponding resolved timestamp;
- terminal `closed` history event;
- `closed_at` equal to that history event;
- terminal history actor equal to the verifier; and
- at least one closure-evidence reference.

The legacy single `resolution` field is rejected to avoid ambiguous evidence once multiple remediation attempts are supported.

## Reviewer findings retained as evidence

The adversarial review found and corrected:

1. **Historical exception expiry bug** — old closed findings would become invalid after an exception expired.
2. **Global exception/history contradiction** — the global validator initially made it impossible to retain an expired exception as audit evidence; `active`/`historical` state fixed the contradiction.
3. **Multiple risk-acceptance overwrite risk** — event-bound exception IDs now preserve separate acceptance periods.
4. **Planned-source overclaim** — SCC and live Terraform drift cannot be represented before their integrations exist.
5. **Ungoverned lifecycle actors and exception approvers** — arbitrary actor/approver strings were removed from the trust model.
6. **Source/gate mismatch** — exception gate is bound to finding source.
7. **Future/retroactive acceptance** — future-created exceptions and acceptance after the remediation SLA are rejected.
8. **Resolution evidence loss** — immutable resolution attempts preserve failed independent-verification evidence across retries.
9. **Closure/history inconsistency** — verifier, verification timestamp, closure timestamp, and terminal lifecycle event are bound together.
10. **Cross-version governance mismatch** — v0.10A's authoritative catalogue validator was updated to explicitly anchor the new active/historical exception contract rather than allowing v0.10C to bypass the earlier governance layer.
